## 1. Technique taxonomy

The controlled vocabulary (38 techniques plus 9 kinds of slow baseline) is in
`research/2026-10-07_technique_mapping.json`. This section explains the techniques that the dataset uses, grouped
by the structural property they exploit. "Entries" lists the ids whose slow-to-fast transformation uses the
technique ((p) = provisional record for an entry added during the session).

### 1.1 The techniques

**A. Reuse of shared subproblems**

1. **memoization-dp — Memoisation / dynamic programming.** Solve each distinct subproblem once and combine the
   stored answers by a recurrence. *Exploits:* optimal substructure, plus a set of distinct subproblems far
   smaller than the recursion tree or the solution space. *Entries:* fibonacci (DP step), edit-distance, lcs,
   longest-increasing-subsequence (quadratic DP), matrix-chain, maximum-subarray (Kadane = one-state DP),
   all-pairs-shortest-paths (p) (Floyd–Warshall shares intermediate-vertex subproblems across sources),
   knapsack (capacity DP, pseudo-polynomial, recorded as a peer), the 4 synthetic linear recurrences.
2. **subset-dp — DP over subsets.** The state is a subset plus O(1) boundary information. *Exploits:* the value
   of a partial solution depends on which elements are used, not on their order (n! → 2^n·poly). *Entries:*
   tsp (Held–Karp), and the slow side of chromatic-number (p) (3^n subset DP).
3. **incremental-reuse — Reuse the previous result.** *Exploits:* nested or additive quantities over overlapping
   ranges. *Entries:* maximum-subarray (running sums), longest-palindromic-substring (p) (expand around centers),
   permanent (Gray-code update inside Ryser).

**B. Decomposition and recombination**

4. **divide-and-conquer.** *Exploits:* a partition with a cheap merge (sorted halves, strip lemma, cross
   inversions). *Entries:* sorting (merge sort), inversion-counting, closest-pair, element-distinctness (p) (via merge sort).
5. **meet-in-the-middle.** *Exploits:* additivity of the objective across a split of the variables, so that
   combining is a one-dimensional search. *Entries:* knapsack (Horowitz–Sahni), discrete-logarithm (baby-step
   giant-step, staging).
6. **bilinear-recursion — fewer multiplications in a fixed-size bilinear identity, applied recursively.**
   *Exploits:* the tensor rank of a small format is below the naive count, and recursion turns rank r of format
   k into exponent log_k r. *Entries:* integer-multiplication (Karatsuba, rank 3 instead of 4),
   matrix-multiplication (Strassen, rank 7 instead of 8). This is the one technique in the dataset whose
   fixed-size discoveries amplify into asymptotic statements (Section 4).

**C. Algebraic structure**

7. **algebraic-transform — FFT / NTT / Walsh–Hadamard / zeta–Möbius.** *Exploits:* the characters of a group
   (or the subset lattice's zeta/Möbius pair) diagonalise the operation, and the transform factorises into
   O(log N) sparse stages. *Entries:* polynomial-multiplication (NTT); also used inside chromatic-number (p) and
   forrelation (p, classical side).
8. **elimination-invariant.** *Exploits:* the target (determinant, rank, solution space) is invariant under
   elementary row operations. *Entries:* determinant (Gaussian elimination); simon (GF(2) post-processing).
9. **inclusion-exclusion.** *Exploits:* a covering-type constraint (every column or vertex used), with cheap
   unconstrained counts on subsets. *Entries:* permanent (Ryser), chromatic-number (p) (Björklund–Husfeldt–Koivisto).
10. **algebraic-characterization — a polynomial identity checked in a small ring.** *Entries:* both primality
    entries (AKS).
11. **invariant-reduction — Euclidean descent.** *Entries:* gcd.
12. **repeated-squaring — doubling in a monoid.** *Exploits:* associativity. *Entries:* modular-exponentiation,
    fibonacci (fast doubling), the synthetic linear recurrences (companion matrix), range-minimum-queries (p)
    (the doubling construction of the sparse table); also inside Miller–Rabin, AKS and Shor.

**D. Order, greed and duality**

13. **greedy-exchange.** *Exploits:* matroid or cut structure (MST), and the finality of minimum labels under
    non-negative weights (Dijkstra). *Entries:* minimum-spanning-tree, shortest-path.
14. **sorting-monotone-scan.** *Exploits:* after sorting, the predicate is monotone in the index, or equal items
    are adjacent. *Entries:* three-sum, longest-increasing-subsequence (patience sorting), element-distinctness (p);
    also inside Kruskal and the knapsack meet in the middle.
15. **primal-dual-potentials** and **augmenting-paths.** *Exploits:* LP integrality and duality; optimality
    holds iff there is no improving path. *Entries:* assignment (Hungarian). Augmenting paths also appear on the
    slow side of bipartite-matching (p) (Kuhn) and max-flow (p) (Edmonds–Karp).
16. **phased-augmentation — blocking flows.** *Exploits:* residual s–t distances grow monotonically from phase
    to phase. *Entries:* bipartite-matching (p) (Hopcroft–Karp), max-flow-edmonds-karp-vs-dinic (p).
17. **continuous-optimization** and **preflow-push.** *Entries:* the LP and max-flow staging entries.

**E. Scanning and data structures**

18. **amortized-scan** and **automaton.** *Exploits:* self-similarity of the pattern (borders, palindromic
    mirrors) and regularity (finitely many relevant states). *Entries:* string-matching (KMP: both),
    longest-palindromic-substring (p) (Manacher: amortized-scan). Only one entry uses `automaton`.
19. **data-structure.** *Entries:* range-minimum-queries (p) (sparse table: min is associative and idempotent).
    Union-find appears only inside Kruskal.

**F. Randomisation (classical)**

20. **random-witness.** *Exploits:* witnesses are abundant. *Entries:* primality-miller-rabin-vs-aks,
    polynomial-identity-testing (staging), deutsch-jozsa (p) (classical randomized side).
21. **random-collision.** *Exploits:* a hidden 2-to-1 structure (the birthday bound). *Entries:* the classical
    side of simon and collision (p); Pollard rho in the staging factoring and discrete-log entries.
22. **randomized-search, random-order, random-sampling-estimation.** *Entries:* 3sat-brute-force-vs-schoening (p)
    (Schöning's walk); boolean-satisfiability (Schöning, PPSZ; staging); the classical simulation side of
    forrelation (p).

**G. Quantum and dequantisation**

23. **quantum-fourier-sampling.** *Exploits:* the hidden object is a character or a coset structure of an
    abelian group. *Entries:* bernstein-vazirani, deutsch-jozsa (p), simon, forrelation (p), and Shor in
    integer-factoring and discrete-logarithm (staging).
24. **amplitude-amplification.** *Entries:* grover, collision (p) (Brassard–Høyer–Tapp), minimum-finding (p)
    (Dürr–Høyer).
25. **quantum-walk** (element-distinctness-quantum-walk, p), **quantum-linear-algebra** (Kerenidis–Prakash;
    linear-systems-hhl, p), **length-squared-sampling** (Tang, T5; also the low-rank classical algorithm in
    linear-systems-hhl, p).
26. **group-theoretic** (graph isomorphism) and **sieving-smoothness** (number field sieve): staging only.

**Vocabulary items that no entry uses:** `branch-and-reduce`, `monotone-matrix-search` and
`determinant-counting` [exp: dataset_stats]. `structured-linear-algebra` appears only as the classical baseline
of linear-systems-hhl (p).

### 1.2 Two kinds of slow baseline, and when memoisation is enough (measured)

The mapping labels 48 algorithms in pairs/ and staging/ with a baseline kind (the slow side; also the
best-known-classical intermediates of the open problems and the quantum-claimed side of the T5 entry)
[exp: dataset_stats]: exhaustive enumeration 20, classical query baseline 8, best known classical algorithm 4,
unmemoised recursion 4, value-linear iteration (polynomial in the value, exponential in the bit length) 4,
direct definition (schoolbook formula) 3, simple polynomial algorithm 3, repeated subroutine 1, quantum-claimed 1.

For the recursive baselines, the decisive structural quantity is the number of distinct subproblems. I counted it
in the unchanged dataset implementations by tracing their recursive calls with `sys.setprofile`
[exp: subproblem_redundancy]:

| Entry (slow implementation) | Memo key | Distinct keys (measured) | Calls at the largest n |
|---|---|---|---|
| fibonacci naive | n | n + 1 (n = 10..22) | 57,313 at n = 22 |
| synthetic linear-recurrence c1-1-1 | n | n + 1 | 433,993 at n = 22 |
| edit-distance plain recursion | (i, j) | (n + 1)² (n = 2..8) | 398,593 at n = 8 (81 keys) |
| matrix-chain plain recursion | (i, j) | n(n + 1)/2 (n = 4..12) | 177,147 = 3^11 at n = 12 (78 keys) |
| determinant cofactor expansion | remaining column set | **2^n exactly** (n = 3..8) | 109,601 at n = 8 (256 keys) |
| shortest-path DFS | (vertex, visited set) | **n·2^(n−3) + 1** (n = 4..9) | 27,400 at n = 9 (577 keys); vertex alone: n keys |

(The closed forms were read off the measured tables, not proven.)

**Pattern.** "Memoise the recurrence" yields a polynomial algorithm only when the distinct keys are polynomially
many: the first four rows. For the determinant, memoisation of the natural key gives about 2^n states. The
polynomial algorithm needs a different property, row-operation invariance. For shortest paths, the polynomial
algorithm needs a *state compression* (drop the visited set), which is valid because non-negative weights make
some shortest walk simple. Both are structural insights beyond memoisation. A learned "un-bloater" that only
memoises would solve the first four rows and fail on the last two. This is a measurable version of the
START_HERE section 5 warning.

### 1.3 Patterns across tags (from the mapping)

Primary tag × transformation technique over pairs/ and staging/ [exp: dataset_stats]:

- **T2 (naive exponential → polynomial):** memoization-dp 5, greedy-exchange 2, repeated-squaring 2, and one
  each of elimination-invariant, invariant-reduction, primal-dual + augmenting-paths, sorting-monotone-scan.
- **T3 (polynomial → faster polynomial):** divide-and-conquer 3; amortized-scan, bilinear-recursion,
  incremental-reuse, memoization-dp, phased-augmentation and sorting-monotone-scan 2 each; algebraic-transform,
  automaton, data-structure, continuous-optimization and preflow-push 1 each.
- **T6 (open problems, most with a T8 secondary):** inclusion-exclusion 2, meet-in-the-middle 2,
  randomized-search 2, subset-dp 1, plus the sub-exponential, quantum, quantum-linear-algebra and group-theoretic
  techniques of the open problems.
- **T9 (quantum query separations):** quantum-fourier-sampling 4, amplitude-amplification 3, quantum-walk 1,
  random-witness 1 (the classical randomized side of Deutsch–Jozsa).

Four of the five T8 pairs in pairs/ (knapsack, permanent, TSP, chromatic number) rely on one of three
properties: order-independence (subset DP), covering structure (inclusion–exclusion) or additive separability
(meet in the middle). The fifth, 3-SAT (Schöning, added during this session), uses randomized local search. The
T8 entries in staging add group theory (graph isomorphism) and collisions and sieving (factoring).

## 5. Pair generators

Modelled on `generators/linear_recurrence.py` (input spec → complete entry: implementations, harness,
`entry.json` with automatically derived cost claims, README). There are two honestly different classes:

- **Mechanical transformations** (class M): un-memoising, bloating, re-instantiating a known template. The output
  is **T7**, lives in `synthetic/` and is never counted (START_HERE section 5). It is useful as verified training
  data for "un-bloating" and as regression tests for the validator.
- **Search with an exact verifier** (class S): the generator *finds* something (a scheme, a rule set, an instance
  family) and verifies it exactly. Its outputs can be T2/T3/T8 entries, but almost always as rediscoveries. A
  genuinely new result needs a literature check and the maintainer's review.
