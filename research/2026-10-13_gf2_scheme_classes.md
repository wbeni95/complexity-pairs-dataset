# Nine GF(2) schemes for ⟨2,5,6⟩ (rank 47) and ⟨3,5,5⟩ (rank 58), compared with the public schemes of these formats

Date: 2026-10-13. Author: delegated research agent (Claude), for the maintainer.

Every number below is printed by `experiments/2026-10-13_gf2_scheme_classes.py`. The console copy of a run on the
pinned public files is `search/runs/2026-10-13_gf2_scheme_classes.console.txt`. Sections 2 and 4 hold the proofs;
the script checks their computable parts.

## Summary

* **Nine schemes** are in `search/schemes/gf2-2x5x6-3x5x5/`: three of rank 47 for ⟨2,5,6⟩ and six of rank 58 for
  ⟨3,5,5⟩. All nine pass both exact verifiers of `search/gf2mm.py`.
* **Same ranks as known schemes.** Public schemes already reach 47 and 58 over GF(2) (section 3). No rank and no
  exponent changes.
* **Inequivalent under the group G** (section 2). Each of the nine is inequivalent to each of the 33 public
  representations of its format listed in section 3. Within each format the nine are also pairwise inequivalent.
  * *Proof:* the invariant κ (section 4) is constant on G-orbits.
  * It takes 9 different values on the nine schemes, and none of them is among the values on the public
    representations: 3 for ⟨2,5,6⟩, 7 for ⟨3,5,5⟩.
* **The 33 public representations are 10 GF(2) schemes.** After reduction mod 2 and orientation to the target
  format, they are 3 distinct GF(2) term sets for ⟨2,5,6⟩ and 7 for ⟨3,5,5⟩. Each κ value of the public files
  belongs to exactly one of these term sets.
* **Not claimed:**
  * novelty in an absolute sense;
  * inequivalence under any group larger than G;
  * anything about other coefficient rings.

## 2. The group G

A scheme of format ⟨n,m,p⟩ over a field F is valid iff Σ_t ⟨U_t,X⟩⟨V_t,Y⟩⟨W_t,Z⟩ = tr(XYZ) for all X (n × m),
Y (m × p) and Z (p × n), where ⟨A,X⟩ = Σ A_xy X_xy. We use the group G of RL-071, which follows Kauers & Moosbauer
(ISSAC 2023, §2). It is generated by:
1. **term permutations;**
2. **sandwiches** (P, Q, S) ∈ GL_n × GL_m × GL_p: (U, V, W) ↦ (PUQ⁻¹, QVS⁻¹, SWP⁻¹);
3. **format maps:**
   * c: (U, V, W) ↦ (V, W, U), from ⟨n,m,p⟩ to ⟨m,p,n⟩;
   * t: (U, V, W) ↦ (Wᵀ, Vᵀ, Uᵀ), from ⟨n,m,p⟩ to ⟨n,p,m⟩.

Over GF(2) a term has no non-trivial rescaling, and the field has no non-trivial automorphism.

**Lemma 2.1 (G maps schemes to schemes).**
* *Sandwich:* ⟨PUQ⁻¹, X⟩ = ⟨U, PᵀXQ⁻ᵀ⟩, and likewise for V and W. So the image satisfies
  Σ_t … = tr(PᵀX·YZ·P⁻ᵀ) = tr(XYZ).
* *c:* the identity becomes tr(YZX) = tr(XYZ).
* *t:* ⟨Wᵀ, X′⟩ = ⟨W, X′ᵀ⟩, and likewise for the other factors. So the identity becomes tr(Z′ᵀY′ᵀX′ᵀ) = tr(X′Y′Z′). ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (normal form).** Words are read left to right: tc means t, then c.
1. On schemes, c³ = id, t² = id and tc = c²t. Both sides of the last identity send (U, V, W) to (Vᵀ, Uᵀ, Wᵀ).
   So c and t generate exactly the six maps id, c, c², t, tc, tc². They send (n,m,p) to (n,m,p), (m,p,n), (p,n,m),
   (n,p,m), (p,m,n) and (m,n,p): the six permutations of the three positions.
2. A format map moves past a sandwich and gives a sandwich:
   * the sandwich (P, Q, S) followed by c equals c followed by the sandwich (Q, S, P);
   * the sandwich (P, Q, S) followed by t equals t followed by the sandwich (P⁻ᵀ, S⁻ᵀ, Q⁻ᵀ) of format ⟨n,p,m⟩.

   Both are checked by multiplying out. Term permutations commute with everything.
3. Hence every element of G is a term permutation composed with a sandwich and one of the six words w, and it maps
   schemes of format f to schemes of format w(f). ∎

**Corollary 2.3 (format self-maps).** An element of G maps schemes of format ⟨n,m,p⟩ to schemes of the same format
iff its word fixes (n,m,p).
* A word other than id fixes (n,m,p) iff two of n, m, p are equal.
  * If n, m, p are distinct, only the identity permutation fixes the triple.
  * If exactly two are equal, only the transposition of their two positions does (besides id).
  * If all three are equal, all six do.
* For ⟨2,5,6⟩ only id fixes the format.
* For ⟨3,5,5⟩ (m = p ≠ n) exactly id and t fix it.
  * t exchanges the slot of U (3 × 5) with the slot of W (5 × 3), up to transposition.
  * The 5 × 5 slot stays in place: V ↦ Vᵀ.
* So on schemes of one of these two formats, G acts through term permutations and sandwiches, together with t for
  ⟨3,5,5⟩. ∎

The script checks the rule for all 216 formats with entries 1..6 and lists the fixing words: `['id']` for (2,5,6)
and `['id', 't']` for (3,5,5). Every word maps the standard algorithm, and each of the nine schemes, to a valid scheme
of the image format.

**What is not claimed.** As in RL-071, G is not claimed to be the full isotropy group of the matrix multiplication
tensor over GF(2). Inequivalence below is relative to G.

**How each representation is read** (`classify` in the script):
1. The SHA-256 of the file must equal the pin.
2. The file is parsed. The readers are for the JSON files, both dense and addition-reduced; Maple `TriadSet`;
   Mathematica lists; Kauers–Moosbauer `.exp`; the AlphaEvolve notebook; and the AlphaTensor `.npz`.
   * The `.npz` members are object arrays in pickle format. They are decoded statically: `pickletools.genops` lists
     the opcodes and only literal arguments are read. Nothing is unpickled.
3. Each third factor is tried in two readings, as W[k][i] and as the output entry (i, k). A representation is
   accepted only if exactly one reading validates: exactly over ℚ, or over GF(2) for the two GF(2)-only
   representations pub23 and pub32. In all 33 cases the valid reading is W[k][i]. Both GF(2)-only representations
   fail the exact check over ℚ in both readings (a control).
4. **Reduction mod 2.** All files have integer coefficients, except pub02 and pub08, whose denominators are 1, 3, 5,
   7, 9, 11, 15, 25, 35 and 55.
   * All denominators are odd, so every coefficient lies in ℤ₍₂₎, the rationals with odd denominator.
   * The Brent equations are polynomial identities with integer coefficients. Reduction ℤ₍₂₎ → GF(2) is a ring
     homomorphism, so it maps a scheme valid over ℚ with such coefficients to a scheme valid over GF(2).
   * The script refuses any even denominator; none occurs.
   * After reduction every representation still has 47 (resp. 58) distinct non-zero terms.
5. The reduced scheme is brought to the target format by every word of Lemma 2.2 that maps its format there:
   * t for 2×6×5;
   * c and tc² for 5×3×5;
   * c² and tc for 5×5×3;
   * id and t for 3×5×5 itself.

   Each image must pass `verify` and `verify_explicit`, and all images must give the same κ.

## 4. The invariant κ

**Definition.** For a scheme S over GF(2) of format ⟨n,m,p⟩, take the GF(2) ranks of the three factors of each term.
* ⟨2,5,6⟩ (n, m, p distinct): κ(S) is the multiset of the triples (rk U, rk V, rk W).
* ⟨3,5,5⟩ (m = p ≠ n): κ(S) is the multiset of the triples (rk V, min(rk U, rk W), max(rk U, rk W)).

**Proposition 4.1.** κ is constant on the G-orbits of schemes of format ⟨2,5,6⟩, and on those of format ⟨3,5,5⟩.
Hence two schemes of the same format with different κ are inequivalent under G.

*Proof.* By Corollary 2.3, an element of G that maps schemes of one of these formats to schemes of the same format
is a composition of term permutations, sandwiches and, for ⟨3,5,5⟩ only, t. Each of these keeps κ:
* A term permutation does not change a multiset.
* A sandwich replaces a term by (PUQ⁻¹, QVS⁻¹, SWP⁻¹) with P, Q, S invertible, so each rank is unchanged.
* t sends the triple of a term to (rk Wᵀ, rk Vᵀ, rk Uᵀ) = (rk W, rk V, rk U). The middle entry stays and the outer
  two are exchanged, so the ⟨3,5,5⟩ key (rk V, min, max) is unchanged. ∎

Equal κ would prove nothing, but no two compared schemes have equal κ.

The script checks the computable part. 30 random sandwiches per scheme (270 images), and t on the six ⟨3,5,5⟩
schemes, give valid schemes with unchanged κ. Without the symmetrisation the triple multiset changes under t for 6 of
the 6 ⟨3,5,5⟩ schemes, so the symmetrised key is needed.

**Statement.** Under G, each of the nine schemes is inequivalent to every public representation in section 3 and
to the other schemes of its format (Proposition 4.1). This adds nine G-classes of GF(2) schemes, three for ⟨2,5,6⟩
and six for ⟨3,5,5⟩, to the 3 + 7 public classes we compared. The ranks are the known ones, so this changes no
exponent.

## 6. Limits

* **Relative to G.** Inequivalence is relative to G (section 2), the group used by Kauers & Moosbauer and in
  RL-071. A larger group could identify more schemes.
* **Only these collections.** The comparison covers the 33 representations of section 3. Collections we did not
  find, or schemes their authors have not published, may contain these schemes. This is not a claim of novelty in an
  absolute sense.
* **GF(2) only.** The nine schemes are schemes over GF(2). Nothing is claimed about their validity over other rings,
  or about lifts.
* **No third-party files here.** The pinned files are referenced by URL and SHA-256 and downloaded into a cache
  outside the repository; none is copied into the repository.

## 7. Reproduction

```
python experiments/2026-10-13_gf2_scheme_classes.py                       # offline: recorded public invariants
python experiments/2026-10-13_gf2_scheme_classes.py --cache DIR --download  # fetch the pinned files into DIR
python experiments/2026-10-13_gf2_scheme_classes.py --cache DIR           # recompute everything from DIR
```

DIR must lie outside the repository. The run with `--cache` reads 33 pinned files and 3 primary copies (36 SHA-256
checks, 59 exact tensor builds over ℚ, 413 calls of `verify` and 83 of `verify_explicit`). It prints 100 [PASS]
lines and no [FAIL] line. The offline run prints 27 [PASS] lines (354 calls of `verify`, 28 of `verify_explicit`).
Each mode gave byte-identical output in two runs.
